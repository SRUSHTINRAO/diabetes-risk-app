🔍 What is it?

Blood glucose is the amount of sugar circulating in your blood. Your body uses glucose as its primary energy source. The pancreas produces insulin to help cells absorb glucose — when this system fails, glucose builds up in the blood, leading to diabetes.

📊 Normal Ranges (Indian Guidelines)
Condition Fasting Post-Meal (2hr)
✅ Normal Below 100 mg/dL Below 140 mg/dL
🟡 Prediabetes 100–125 mg/dL 140–199 mg/dL
🔴 Diabetes 126+ mg/dL 200+ mg/dL
⚠️ Why context matters

A glucose of 130 mg/dL is concerning if fasting (above the 126 diabetes threshold) but normal after a meal (below 140). Always note when your blood sugar was measured.

💡 How to improve it
🥗
Replace white rice and maida with ragi, jowar or brown rice — they digest slower and cause smaller blood sugar spikes.
🚶
A 20-minute walk after meals significantly lowers post-meal glucose — more effective than walking at other times.
💧
Stay well hydrated — dehydration concentrates blood sugar. Replace sugary drinks with water or buttermilk.
😴
Poor sleep raises blood sugar. Even one night of bad sleep can spike fasting glucose by 10–15%.

🔍 What is it?

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. Higher BMI increases insulin resistance — a key driver of Type 2 diabetes.

📊 Normal Ranges

Note: Indian guidelines use lower thresholds than Western standards.

Category Asian BMI Western BMI
✅ Normal 18.5–22.918.5–24.9
🟡 Overweight 23–27.425–29.9
🔴 Obese 27.5+30+
💡 How to improve it
🎯
Even losing 3–5% of body weight significantly reduces diabetes risk.
🍽️
Use smaller plates and eat slowly — it takes 20 minutes for your brain to register fullness.
🏋️
Strength training builds muscle which burns more calories at rest.
🥤
Cutting out sugary drinks alone can lead to 2–3 kg weight loss over 3 months.
🔍 What is it?

Blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls. This app uses the diastolic value (the lower number) which is more sensitive to early cardiovascular risk.

📊 Normal Ranges
Category Diastolic (mm Hg)
✅ Normal60–80
🟡 Elevated80–89
🔴 High (Stage 1)90–99
🔴 High (Stage 2)100+
🔗 Link to diabetes

High blood pressure and diabetes frequently co-occur. Having both dramatically increases risk of kidney disease, stroke and heart attack.

💡 How to improve it
🧂
Reduce salt intake — most Indians consume 2–3x the recommended amount.
🚶
Regular aerobic exercise (brisk walking 30 min daily) can lower diastolic BP by 4–9 mm Hg over weeks.
🧘
Chronic stress raises BP. Even 10 minutes of deep breathing or meditation daily has measurable effects.
🔍 What is it?

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that allows cells to absorb glucose. In Type 2 diabetes, cells become resistant to insulin.

📊 Normal Range
Status Fasting Insulin (µU/mL)
✅ Normal16–166
🟡 LowBelow 16
🔴 Low (insulin resistance)Above 166
⚠️ Zero value note

Many people don't know their insulin level — if you entered 0, the model uses dataset averages. Getting your fasting insulin tested gives a much more accurate risk picture.

💡 How to improve insulin sensitivity
🏃
Exercise is the most powerful way to improve insulin sensitivity.
🫘
Eat more legumes and fibre — dal, rajma, chana slow digestion and reduce insulin spikes after meals.
⏰
Intermittent fasting (eating within a 10-hour window) can significantly improve insulin sensitivity over weeks.
🔍 What is it?

Skin thickness (triceps skinfold) estimates subcutaneous body fat. Higher skinfold thickness indicates higher body fat percentage, which is linked to insulin resistance.

📊 Normal Ranges
Category Range (mm)
✅ Normal10–40
🔵 Low (may be unmeasured)Below 10
🟡 ElevatedAbove 40
⚠️ Practical note

Most people don't have access to skinfold calipers. If you don't know this value, enter 20 (near population average).

💡 How to reduce body fat
🎯
Combination of cardio and strength training reduces body fat more effectively than either alone.
🥗
Prioritize protein and fibre at every meal — they keep you full longer.
🔍 What is it?

The Diabetes Pedigree Function (DPF) quantifies genetic risk based on family history. A higher score means stronger inherited risk.

📊 Score Ranges
Risk Level DPF Score
✅ LowBelow 0.3
🟡 Moderate0.3–0.6
🔴 HighAbove 0.6
🧮 How to estimate your score
Family History Approximate DPF
No family history0.1–0.2
One grandparent with diabetes0.2–0.4
One parent with diabetes0.4–0.6
Both parents with diabetes0.6–1.0
Multiple close relativesAbove 1.0
💡 What you can do about genetic risk
🧬
Genetics loads the gun but lifestyle pulls the trigger. Studies show lifestyle changes reduce diabetes risk by 58% even in high genetic-risk groups.
🩺
If you have a strong family history, start annual screening at age 25.
👨‍👩‍👧
Share your family history with your doctor — it changes their screening approach.
🔍 Why age matters

Diabetes risk increases with age due to gradual decline in insulin sensitivity, muscle mass loss, and reduced physical activity. India is seeing rapidly increasing rates in young adults (25–44) due to lifestyle changes.

📊 Risk by age group (India)
Age Group Risk Level Recommendation
Under 30 🟢 Lower Screen if family history
30–44 🟡 Increasing Screen every 2 years
45–59 🔴 High Screen annually
60+ 🔴 Very High Screen every 6 months
💡 Age-specific tips
💪
After 40, muscle mass naturally declines — add strength training to preserve it. More muscle means better glucose metabolism.
🩺
After 45, get a comprehensive metabolic panel annually — glucose, HbA1c, cholesterol, kidney function, liver function.
🔍 Why it matters

Gestational diabetes (diabetes during pregnancy) is a strong predictor of later Type 2 diabetes. Women who had gestational diabetes have a 7–10x higher lifetime risk.

📊 Risk by pregnancy count
Pregnancies Risk Level

⚠️ Note for men

Enter 0 if you are male — this field will have no impact on your prediction.

💡 What to do if you had gestational diabetes
🩺
Get tested for diabetes 6–12 weeks after delivery and then every 1–3 years.
🥗
Breastfeeding, maintaining healthy weight and regular exercise after pregnancy significantly reduce your long-term risk.
